About p5.penplotter

A p5 sketch runs sixty times a second and forgets every frame. A pen plotter draws once and keeps it. I wanted the smallest possible bridge between those two habits, and this is it.

Why

The usual way to plot a p5 sketch is to record it as an SVG, clean it up in vpype, open it in Inkscape and send it from there. Four programs for one drawing. p5.penplotter replaces them with one object: every plot.line(), plot.circle() or plot.hatch() draws on the canvas and is remembered in millimetres, and plot.go() hands the last frame to the machine. The sketch stays a sketch; draw() still runs, random() still rolls, and the plot is always the snapshot of what you last saw.

Three commands, no thinking of its own

p5.penplotter gives p5 three new commands, createPlot(), createPlotterEngine() and drawRoute(), in global and instance mode, and that is all. Everything that takes thought lives in vanilla.penplotter, which knows nothing about p5: the shapes in millimetres, the tidying, the route for the pen, the files for other machines, the facts about the plotter and the talking to it. When something is fixed there, it is fixed for everyone at once. The one thing p5.penplotter does decide is where your canvas lands on the paper and how wide: createPlot({ x, y, width }) says so, and from then on every pixel has its millimetre.

The dependency runs one way, and it is checked: when a driver is handed in, p5.penplotter reads the engine's version and refuses one that is too old, with a plain sentence. The Requirements table is identical in both READMEs because a test says so.

Not p5.plotSvg

p5.plotSvg by Golan Levin records a p5 sketch between beginRecordSvg() and endRecordSvg() and writes an SVG. It covers far more of p5's drawing API than this adapter: curves, text, arcs in every mode. It deliberately stops at the file. p5.penplotter chose the other end of the road: fewer primitives, but no file. The two are not rivals; a p5.plotSvg file can be read and plotted by the engine's SVG to pen example.

The machine

Everything so far has been plotted on one machine, mine: an iDraw HSE with an A2 bed and an EBB board, over Web Serial from Chrome. The machine facts, measured on paper on 2026-09-21, live in the engine. The sketch only needs to know that the home corner is 0,0, that the carriage is parked there by hand, and that plot.go() has to come from a key or a click, because a browser shows its port list only after a user gesture.

How this was made

I use AI to build bridges, not cities. This adapter is the smallest bridge I could make: three methods between p5.js and an engine that already existed, so that a sketch reaches a pen. The conversations that built it connected things I could not connect before. They never replaced the pleasure of coding; that stays mine, and so does the responsibility for what is here.

I designed and directed it, wrote it with AI assistance, and held it to one rule: nothing is claimed that a test or a plot on paper has not shown. The commit history is in my name only. The Setup page exists because getting a module-based adapter into a global-mode sketch is the one thing that went wrong more than once.
